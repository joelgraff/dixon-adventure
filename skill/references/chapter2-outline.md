# Chapter 2 outline: Dixon's Ferry, May 1832 (draft for review)

First draft, 2026-11-08. Nothing here is canon until the user approves it.
Facts and their sources are in research-1832.md; decisions are in
future-hooks.md ("Chapter 2 break").

## Decided
- **The break:** Dixon leaves (option 1). On record he nearly did, and stayed
  on Crane's assurance.
- **The night the band comes:** the family-history version. John Dixon is away
  at Galena. Rebecca is home with the children when Black Hawk's band crowds
  into the house; she sends for Old Crane (Pachinka), who with the Sauk chief
  Wischick clears the house; at Crane's suggestion she cooks for Black Hawk,
  Wischick and Kaapapi while the band camps at the spring.
- **The third traveler's motive:** she knows that two of the Dixons' small
  children die at Galena, where Rebecca takes them for safety. She means to
  save them.
- **The Margaret line:** "cash to Margaret to go to Fort $2", from Ogee's
  account in Dixon's book, is used. Its link to our Margaret is invented and is
  marked so in sources.yaml.

## The shape (proposed)

**The break, as it happened.** The third traveler spent the winter at the
ferry as the Dixons' hired help (the record mentions "his family and hired
help"). The children loved her, and she knitted them mittens. She knew the
dates. She couldn't stop the war, but she could keep the family off the
Galena road. Rebecca won't take the children anywhere without John ("I am not
doing it backwards", chapter 1), so the traveler went to work on John. Before
the band came she was gone: the moment was decided, and the fog took her (the
fog rule: a traveler goes home once the moment is *decided*, right or wrong,
as in chapter 1's wrong fix). What she left behind is **a letter for John**,
waiting at the post office for him to come home from Galena: her warning,
written out in more detail than anyone in 1832 could know (Stillman's Run,
the killings, the dead children). It frightens a man who was already
wavering. He takes his family east, the ferry is abandoned, Atkinson's army
finds an empty landing, the base and Fort Dixon go somewhere else, and Dixon
is never "Father Dixon" of the war.

**What the player must restore.**
1. The night of the band: help Rebecca through it (Crane has to be sent for,
   and the player can be the one who goes), so that Crane's word, and the
   chiefs' gratitude for her table, reach John when he comes home.
2. John's choice: he stays at the ferry. Crane's assurance against the
   traveler's warning.
3. The family's road: Rebecca and the children go to **Galena**, as on record.
   This is the Edith Keeler beat. The player knows what happens there; so does
   the traveler. Restoring the record means letting them go.

**The traveler (decided 2026-11-08: gone before the player arrives, glimpsed
once).** The player never meets her in chapter 2. As the player comes out of
the fog on arrival, a woman walks into it the other way with something lit in
her hand, and is gone. After that she exists only in what she left: the
Dixons' memories of the woman who worked for them all winter and left in the
fog, the bed she slept in, the children's mittens, and her letter. Her ending
is deferred to a later chapter; meeting her is a climax for later. Possible
link to Margaret: her handwriting looks like Margaret's "careful hand".

**The letter is the puzzle.** The player is up against a piece of paper in
John's hands, set against Crane's word and Rebecca's night. The letter was
never part of the record, so keeping it from doing its work (finding it
first, answering it, or letting Crane's assurance outweigh it) is a repair in
itself.

**Present-day wrongness at the start of the chapter** (after chapter 1's
repair, before chapter 2's): candidates
- The Lincoln statue on North Galena Avenue, which marks the Fort Dixon site
  and carries John Dixon's name on its base, isn't there (or stands somewhere
  else, with no Dixon on it).
- Dixon's account book in the museum case stops in April 1832; the second
  book, the war book, doesn't exist.
- Margaret's "the militia came through here in 1832, Lincoln among them" turns
  into something vaguer.
- *Decided: both the statue and the account book.*

**The drawer's object (the ticket).** *Decided: a child's mitten, one of the
pair the third traveler knitted for the Dixon children.* It looks random in a
drawer opened in spring; it comes to mean the children, and her. Earlier
candidates, for the record:
- a child's mitten, small, in a drawer opened in spring (the account book has
  "2 pr. mittens for sons" on Ogee's account; it would come to mean the Dixon
  children);
- a blue glass trade bead (Crane's account: blue cloth, beads);
- ~~a scorched iron hinge from a ferry~~ (ruled out).

**Margaret in chapter 2.** The account book in the museum case has "cash to
Margaret to go to Fort $2". She reads it out, laughs at seeing her own name,
and then stops laughing. Her rising awareness starts here.

## Cast (May 1832)
- **Rebecca Dixon** (centre of the chapter): tired, dry, not going backwards.
- **John Dixon**: away at Galena with the mail; comes home to a decision.
- **James** (21) and **Elijah** (about 15): on record they kept the house
  open while the family was away. James still has the mail key.
- **Old Crane (Pachinka)**, Ho-Chunk chief, Dixon's friend and customer; and
  **Wischick** (Sauk). Historical people: few words each, with dignity, never
  scenery.
- **Black Hawk and Kaapapi**: present at the dinner; brief. Black Hawk's own
  account (his 1833 autobiography) can be quoted, since it is public domain.
- **The third traveler**: absent, glimpsed once in the fog on arrival. Present
  through her letter, her bed, the children's mittens and what the family
  says of her. Her letter can carry the travelers' phrase ("I decided not to
  think about it").
- **Returning from 1830:** Asa (hauling to Galena: the family could ride with
  his wagons), Eli (militia age; Stillman's battalion rides out from the ferry
  on May 13), the crow with Asa's dollar, Madeline's paddle under the eaves.
  Ogee sold the ferry in January 1832; *decided: he and Madeline are still
  about.*
- **Cameos:** the army arrives about May 10-12 (Governor Reynolds, Whiteside,
  Captain Lincoln's company camped a mile west).

## Option 2 in reserve
If the middle needs a set piece: with the family gone and the landing empty,
the ferry burns (Bogardus's boat did; Madeline said nobody would burn this
one). Rebuilding the crossing before the army arrives reuses chapter 1's
skills and objects.

## Decided 2026-11-08 (second round)
- **Present-day wrongness: both.** The Lincoln statue is missing and Dixon's
  account book stops in April 1832.
- **The drawer object:** the mitten, and it is hers (see above).
- **Ogee and Madeline are at the ferry in 1832.** (Ogee sold the ferry to
  Dixon in January 1832; why they are still about is for the scenes to say.)

## Open questions
1. **The children.** Either nothing the player does can save them, or the
   player can save them without breaking Dixon's record, and that becomes a
